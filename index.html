About

I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science at Texas State University, where I work as a Doctoral Research Assistant. My research is on GPU performance for irregular workloads, mainly graph analytics.

Part of my work is CUDA kernel optimization, rewriting graph traversal kernels with warp-level primitives and profiling-driven tuning. The other part looks at GPU partitioning: measuring how small a slice of a GPU a graph workload can run on before performance drops, so one card can serve several jobs at once.

Before the PhD I completed an MS in Electrical Engineering at Texas State and worked as an IoT Specialist in Dhaka. I program mainly in C/C++, CUDA and Python, and I use Nsight Systems, Nsight Compute and VTune for performance analysis.

Research

GPU right-sizing for irregular workloads

Dissertation work, in progress

Most graph workloads do not need a whole GPU. I time cuGraph BFS, PageRank and SCC across the full GPU and the MIG partitions of an A100 80GB, over a corpus of more than 1,200 graphs, and look for the smallest partition that still runs the job at close to full speed.

  • A placement rule that trades speed against utilization keeps most of the full-GPU BFS performance while using roughly a quarter of the card.
  • A tree-ensemble model predicts the right partition from static structural features of a graph alone, such as degree distribution, diameter, triangle counts and spectral properties, so a job can be placed without profiling it first.
  • Full hardware-counter profiling was impractical per graph, so I built a sampled two-pass method that recovers utilization within a small error at a fraction of the cost.

Warp-level optimization of CUDA graph kernels

I re-engineered published GPU graph kernels using warp-shuffle reductions, warp-aggregated atomics and tuned launch configurations, then measured them across large benchmark suites.

  • BFS and SSSP rewritten with warp-shuffle reductions, improving on roughly 95% of tested graphs against the published baseline.
  • A Borŭvka MST kernel reworked to pack edge weight and ID into a sortable 64-bit key for a deterministic atomicMin, cutting global atomics to one lane per warp and replacing the per-round edge rescan with an edge-to-source map. Throughput improved on roughly 90% of 1,244 graphs.
  • A connected-components kernel with warp-broadcast neighbour scanning and a virtual-warp width chosen at runtime from average degree.

Projects

Parallel algorithms on GPU clusters

Converted serial algorithms into MPI, pthreads, OpenMP, CUDA, multi-GPU and hybrid implementations, and measured them on GPU cluster nodes through Slurm. A persistent-thread minimum reduction reached about 24.6× over a single-threaded CPU baseline, and a GPU counting sort ran roughly 102× to 136× faster than std::sort across the sizes tested.

Pop-CCL: popularity-adaptive long-tail recommendation

Extended the SimpleX collaborative filtering model with a popularity-adaptive Cosine Contrastive Loss, raising Recall@20 for tail items by up to 56% on Yelp18, Amazon-Books and Gowalla with minimal change to the implementation.

Privacy-preserving federated learning

Implemented federated learning with Paillier homomorphic encryption on CIFAR-100 using the AIJack framework, keeping client updates encrypted through aggregation.

Parallel dead-block analysis for C compilers

Sped up dead-block detection with dynamic load balancing and parallel execution across GCC and LLVM builds at varied optimization levels, using Csmith for code generation and program markers for instrumentation.
